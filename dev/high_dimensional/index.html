High-dimensional curves: comparison with Fred

This page compares FrechetDist with Fred (Dennis Rohde's C++ library with Python bindings, version 1.14.5) on real curves in ℝ^963, ℝ^6000 and ℝ^19200: the continuous Fréchet distance between every pair of curves in three datasets. The scripts are in example_higher/ of the repository; see Reproducing the results.

In short:

  • Faster. On one thread, FrechetDist's exact algorithm takes 6.8 s for the 91 traffic pairs (Fred: 110 s), 41 s for the 66 video pairs (Fred: 688 s), and 24 minutes for the 15 hydraulic pairs (Fred: 6.3 hours): 16 to 17 times faster than Fred. On all 16 hardware threads of the machine it takes 1.3 s, 4.8 s and 160 s: 85, 143 and 142 times faster than Fred.
  • Correct where Fred is not. Fred returns a distance that is too low for 22 of the 172 pairs, by up to 3%, although it was asked for 10⁻⁹ relative accuracy. No matching of the two curves achieves the distance it returns. FrechetDist's results agree with an independent decision procedure.
  • Exact. frechet_c_compute_exact has no tolerance: it returns the exact distance (a critical value of Alt and Godau, computed in floating point). frechet_c_compute returns the leash of an actual matching, with a certified lower bound, to a chosen tolerance; at the default 10⁻⁵ it takes 7.0 s and 24.7 s for the traffic and video sets, 9 and 14 times faster than Fred at the same tolerance.

  • Freeway traffic (PEMS-SF, CC BY 4.0). Occupancy rates of 963 freeway sensors in the San Francisco Bay Area, every 10 minutes. Each day (2008–2009) is a curve: 144 vertices, one per 10 minutes, each holding the 963 sensor readings. The first 14 days are used.
  • Video (KTH; Schuldt, Laptev and Caputo, ICPR 2004). Grayscale 160×120 video at 25 frames per second. Each frame is a vertex, its 19 200 pixels scaled to [0, 1]. A curve is the first repetition of an action in a video (64 to 150 frames), outdoors, for persons walking and boxing; the first 12 curves are used.
  • Hydraulic sensors (UCI). Six pressure sensors PS1–PS6 of a hydraulic test rig. Each vertex is one machine cycle, its 6000 samples (60 s at 100 Hz) the coordinates. These are the curves of Meintrup, Munteanu and Rohde, Random Projections and Sampling Algorithms for Clustering of High-Dimensional Polygonal Curves (NeurIPS 2019), who report "roughly three hours" for a sequential implementation of Alt and Godau's algorithm.

What is compared

frechet_c_compute(P, Q, true, tol)a matching (Morphing) whose leash is the distance, and a certified lower boundrelative tol (10⁻⁹ or the default 10⁻⁵)one
Fred continuous_frecheta valueasked for continuous_frechet_error percent (10⁻⁷ or 10⁻³, i.e. 10⁻⁹ or 10⁻⁵ relative)one

frechet_c_compute_exact implements the randomized algorithm of Har-Peled and Raichel, "The Fréchet distance revisited and extended" (Section 6), for two curves. The distance is one of the critical values of Alt and Godau (vertex–vertex, vertex–segment and monotonicity events), the smallest one that the free-space decision procedure accepts:

  1. It tabulates, for every vertex of one curve and every segment of the other, the closest point on the segment's line and the squared distance to it, and all vertex–vertex distances: O(nmd) time, once, on all threads.
  2. It draws 4N² random critical values from the tables (N the larger number of vertices), sorts them, and binary searches them with the decision procedure. This leaves an interval holding the distance and, with high probability, only O(N log N) critical values.
  3. It lists the critical values in that interval. The monotonicity values come from a kinetic sweep along each segment, which keeps the intersection points of the growing spheres in a balanced search tree (O(log N) per event).
  4. It binary searches the list. Each decision reads the tables, O(nm) time.

The result does not depend on the random choices, bit for bit; only the running time does. frechet_c_decide_exact(P, Q, r), the decision procedure (is the distance at most r?), is exported too.

frechet_c_compute computes lower and upper bounds that meet: it simplifies the curves, computes the VE-retractable Fréchet distance between the simplifications (a lower bound) and a monotone matching (an upper bound), and refines until the two agree to the tolerance. Its result is the leash of an actual matching, which it returns, so it is never below the true distance.

Fred computes the continuous Fréchet distance by a binary search with the Alt–Godau decision procedure, stopping when its bounds are within continuous_frechet_error percent.

Results

AMD Ryzen 7 5700G (8 cores, 16 hardware threads), 62 GB, Julia 1.13.3, Python 3.14.8, Fred 1.14.5. Times are for all pairs of a set, excluding compilation (about 3 s, once for all dimensions). "Wrong" counts the pairs more than 10⁻⁸ relative away from the exact distance.

  • frechet_c_compute keeps its tolerance: every result is within 10⁻⁵ of the exact distance (worst +8.7·10⁻⁶), and never below it.
  • Fred does not: the same 7 traffic and 13 video pairs as at 10⁻⁹ are too low by more than 10⁻⁵, by up to 3%.
  • The hydraulic curves take frechet_c_compute the same time at both tolerances (227.5 s and 228.6 s), and at both its results agree with the exact distances to 10⁻¹³.

Notes

  • Fred's errors are all too low. The distance Fred returns for these pairs is not the leash of any matching: the decision procedure rejects it. For the hydraulic pair PS1–PS2, Fred returns 6083.53; the distance is 6096.29.
  • Two independent checks. On the traffic and video sets the exact distances agree with example_higher/check_frechet.jl, a textbook Alt–Godau decision procedure written from scratch (no shared code), to 5·10⁻¹⁴ relative. On the hydraulic set, frechet_c_compute and frechet_c_compute_exact agree to 10⁻¹³.
  • Per pair. On the hydraulic curves the exact algorithm takes 55–114 s per pair on one thread and 5.5–14.6 s on 16 threads, where Fred takes 18–37 minutes. With 2205 vertices per curve, tabulating the vertex–segment distances (O(nmd)) is most of the work; it is the part that runs on all threads.
  • The paper's experiment. Meintrup, Munteanu and Rohde report "roughly three hours" for their sequential Alt–Godau implementation on the hydraulic curves, and about 30 s with random projections and CUDA on Nvidia K40 GPUs. Their hardware and implementations differ, and they do not say whether the times are per distance or for all 15.

Code

High-dimensional curves should use Vector points (GPntSeq{AnyDim,Float64,Vector{Float64}}): the code is compiled once for every dimension (static points, Point{D}, are compiled for each D, which takes minutes at D = 6000). A matrix with one column per vertex converts directly:

using FrechetDist
using BasicCompGeometry: AnyDim, GPntSeq

const Curve = GPntSeq{AnyDim,Float64,Vector{Float64}}
curve(M::Matrix{Float64}) = Curve([M[:, j] for j in axes(M, 2)])  # D × n: one column per vertex

P, Q = curve(A), curve(B)

d = frechet_c_compute_exact(P, Q)        # the exact distance
frechet_c_decide_exact(P, Q, d)          # true: some matching has leash d

m = frechet_c_compute(P, Q)              # tolerance 1e-5 (the default)
m.leash, m.lower_bound                   # the distance is between these two
m = frechet_c_compute(P, Q, true, 1e-9)  # tighter

Start Julia with -t auto (or set JULIA_NUM_THREADS=auto) for the exact algorithm to use all cores; the result is the same for any number of threads.

The same distance with Fred, as example_higher/fred_curves.py runs it:

import Fred
Fred.config.continuous_frechet_error = 1e-7   # percent: 1e-9 relative
Fred.config.number_threads = 1
Fred.continuous_frechet(Fred.Curve(A), Fred.Curve(B)).value   # A, B: n × D arrays

Reproducing the results

From the repository root (the examples have their own environment, example_higher/Project.toml; see example_higher/README.md for installing Fred):

ruby example_higher/prepare_pems.rb                    # traffic: download, convert
ruby example_higher/prepare_kth.rb                     # video: download, convert (needs ffmpeg)
ruby example_higher/download_hydraulic.rb              # hydraulic sensors

python example_higher/fred_curves.py pems              # Fred: all pairs of days 1-14
python example_higher/fred_curves.py kth 1 12
python example_higher/fred_hydraulic.py 2205           # 6.3 hours

julia -t 1 example_higher/curves.jl pems               # frechet_c_compute, tolerance 1e-9
julia -t 1 example_higher/curves.jl kth 1 12
julia -t 1 example_higher/hydraulic.jl 2205

julia -t auto example_higher/exact_curves.jl pems      # frechet_c_compute_exact, and the
julia -t auto example_higher/exact_curves.jl kth 1 12  # errors of the others against it
julia -t auto example_higher/exact_curves.jl hydraulic

julia example_higher/check_frechet.jl pems 1 14        # independent check (slow)

The environment variable FRECHET_TOL (default 1e-9) sets the relative tolerance of curves.jl, hydraulic.jl and fred_curves.py (Fred: 100 × FRECHET_TOL percent); results at other tolerances go to their own files.
